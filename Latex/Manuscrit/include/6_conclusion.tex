\chapter{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}

This thesis addressed autonomous navigation on physical fields when neither satellite positioning nor a reliable embedded map can be taken for granted. The guiding idea has been to reconstruct the observation function of the field with Gaussian processes and to embed this reconstruction within a Bayesian, particle-based navigation filter, so that a single probabilistic model describes both the uncertainty of the state and the uncertainty of the map.

The foundations laid in the first part recalled the Bayesian filters that cope with the non-linear and multimodal densities of terrain-aided navigation, from the Kalman filter to the regularised and Rao-Blackwellised particle filters. They also recalled the Gaussian-process methods that turn field data into an observation function endowed with its own uncertainty, namely kriging, its reduced-rank approximations and the reproducing-kernel-Hilbert-space viewpoint. The second part brought the two together. Kriging was integrated into the particle filter, yielding a navigation scheme that no longer requires a dense and exact embedded map but reconstructs the field from whatever data are available, and that feeds the uncertainty of this reconstruction back into the state estimate. To keep the reconstruction affordable and to respect the non-stationarity of real fields, an adaptive kriging scheme restricts it to the data relevant to the current region, the resulting subsampling error being bounded theoretically. Finally, for the case of an altogether unknown field, localisation and mapping were carried out jointly, by a Rao-Blackwellised filter and by a reproducing-kernel-Hilbert-space reconstruction from a dictionary of basis functions, and the two approaches were compared on a common scenario.

On the scenario of \cref{sec:kriging_navigation}, kriging and bilinear interpolation reconstruct the field with similar errors. The final position error of the kriging-based filter is $1.4$ to $1.9$ times lower, and its NEES stays within the consistency band, whereas that of bilinear interpolation is $9$ to $56$ times higher. Adaptive kriging preserves this accuracy and this consistency while being $213$ to $629$ times less costly than static kriging, its whole mission requiring $46.6$ Gflop on the finest grid. Among the non-spherical windows, the nearest neighbour selection further divides the cost of the kriging by more than ten on this grid without any loss of accuracy, whereas the clustering step brings no gain in accuracy, the particle set being rarely multimodal on this scenario.

Without any prior data, neither SLAM method can correct the initial position error, but both limit its growth. On the closed circuit of \cref{sec:SLAM_results}, RBPF-SLAM keeps an ARMSE of $1.8$ km, against $2.7$ km for the inertial solution, provided that its basis resolves the shortest wavelength of the field. AKKF-SLAM reaches the same accuracy, with an ARMSE of $1.78$ km, and $45$ times less memory, but $12$ times more operations.

\section{Perspectives and Future Work}

The work presented in this document is by no means exhaustive, and can be expanded in different directions. A natural prospect is the application of the developed methods to real data. In particular, several simplifying assumptions made in this document, such as the absence of sensor biases or the time invariance of the field, need to be addressed. Even in simulation, the biases neglected in \cref{sec:kriging_navigation,sec:SLAM} could already be estimated jointly with the state by an RBPF.

Another lead is the fusion of multiple physical fields to improve the accuracy of the estimation. Their combination could resolve the multimodal likelihoods that a single field leaves ambiguous, extending the reconstruction pursued throughout this document from one field to several. The resulting gain could be quantified through the Fisher information matrix \cite{bib:bathymetry-gravimetry}.

On the modelling side, adaptive kriging rests on a local-stationarity heuristic, which a spatially varying set of hyperparameters would relax. Moreover, the training inputs are assumed to be exact, whether they are the positions of a survey or the estimated positions at which AKKF-SLAM learns its map. Accounting for their uncertainty would benefit both scenarios, and could reduce the overconfidence of AKKF-SLAM.

The SLAM methods of \cref{sec:SLAM} could also be improved in two ways. On the one hand, starting the mapping from a region already kriged from prior data would make the initial position observable, thus reducing the uncertainty of the estimation during the first steps of the mission. It would also link the scenarios of the two chapters. On the other hand, detecting loop closures, when the vehicle crosses a region it has already observed, would allow the past trajectory and the map learnt along it to be corrected, which would ensure the consistency of the map \cite{bib:SLAM-survey}.

Finally, the stationarity of the field required in \cref{sec:SLAM} cannot be assumed over areas larger than those considered in this document. To alleviate this issue, the original method of \cite{bib:magnetic-SLAM-3D} divides the domain into tiles, but this introduces artificial boundaries that skew the estimation. These boundaries could be softened by letting the tiles partially overlap, or by combining them through a continuous map of weights. Alternatively, the forgetting factor $\beta_{\mathrm{RLS}}$ of AKKF-SLAM could be used to discard the oldest observations, which generally come from distant regions, so that the local map is not biased by distant data.
