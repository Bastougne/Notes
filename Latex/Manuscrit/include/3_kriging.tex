\section{Kriging}\label{sec:kriging}

Gaussian process regression, also known as kriging \cite{bib:Matheron,bib:Rasmussen-regression,bib:GP-intro}, estimates the value of a field at an unobserved location using noisy measurements of the same field at known locations. Formally, let $f\sim\mathcal{GP}(m,k):\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_z}$, and $x\in\mathcal{U}$. We wish to estimate the law of:
\begin{equation}
	z\triangleq f(x)+\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{d_z},\qquad\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,R),\qquad R\in\mathcal{S}_{d_z}^+(\mathbb{R})
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon$ is independent of $f$.

While kriging usually aims to reconstruct $f(x)$ itself, the query noise $\varepsilon$ models the observation uncertainty, so that kriging estimates the noisy observation from \eqref{eq:navigation_problem}. The standard formulation can nonetheless be recovered by taking $R=0$.

Without further information regarding $f$, we would only be able to state that $z$ follows a Gaussian distribution of expectation $m(x)$ and covariance $k(x,x)+R$. However, we are given $\tilde{n}\in\mathbb{N}^*$ distinct training points $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\triangleq(\tilde{x}_1,\dots,\tilde{x}_{\tilde{n}})\in\mathcal{U}^{\tilde{n}}$ and their corresponding noisy observations:
\begin{equation}
	\tilde{\mathbf{z}}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}
		f(\tilde{x}_1)+\varepsilon_1\\\vdots\\f(\tilde{x}_{\tilde{n}})+\varepsilon_{\tilde{n}}
	\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{\tilde{n}d_z},\qquad\varepsilon_i\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\tilde{R}),\qquad\tilde{R}\in\mathcal{S}_{d_z}^+(\mathbb{R})
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_{\tilde n}$ are mutually independent and independent of $f$.

The goal of this section is to derive the best linear unbiased estimator (BLUE) of $z$ with respect to $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}$, \textit{i.e.} the estimator:
\begin{equation}
	\hat{z}=W^\top\tilde{\mathbf{z}}
\end{equation}
parametrised by a weight matrix:
\begin{equation}
	W\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}
	w_1\\\vdots\\w_{\tilde{n}}
	\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal{M}_{\tilde{n}d_z,d_z}(\mathbb{R})
\end{equation}
such that the error vector has an expectation of $0$ and a minimum covariance. This setting is illustrated by \Cref{fig:kriging_example} for a $\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ field.

\begin{figure}[!htb]
	\centering\includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{../../Images/Exemple de krigeage 3D.pdf}
	\caption{Ordinary kriging (red mesh) of an unknown field (blue surface) from noisy samples (black crosses). The confidence band (red envelope) is proportional to the square root of the interpolation covariance, and widens away from the samples.}\label{fig:kriging_example}
\end{figure}

Since $\hat{z}-z$ is a vector, its covariance is a matrix, and cannot be minimised as such. To alleviate this issue, we minimise its trace, which is exactly the mean squared error:
\begin{equation}
	\operatorname{tr}(\mathbb{V}[\hat{z}-z])=\mathbb{E}\left[\|\hat{z}-z\|^2\right]\label{eq:error_covariance}
\end{equation}

In the following, we present three kriging estimates, based on increasingly relaxed assumptions made on our knowledge of the underlying GP. Each method relies on the inversion of the Gram matrix:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
	G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})&\triangleq k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{x}})+I_{\tilde{n}}\otimes\tilde{R}\\
	&=\begin{bmatrix}
	k(\tilde{x}_1,\tilde{x}_1)+\tilde{R}&\cdots&k(\tilde{x}_1,\tilde{x}_{\tilde{n}})\\\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\k(\tilde{x}_{\tilde{n}},\tilde{x}_1)&\cdots&k(\tilde{x}_{\tilde{n}},\tilde{x}_{\tilde{n}})+\tilde{R}
	\end{bmatrix}
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
of $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}$ with respect to $f$, where $\otimes$ is the Kronecker matrix product. Since $k$ is SPD and the training points are distinct, it comes that:
\begin{equation}
	G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\in\mathcal{S}_{\tilde{n}d_z}^{++}(\mathbb{R})
\end{equation}
and so $G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})$ is invertible, even in the noiseless case $\tilde{R}=0$.

We also assume that the query point $x$ differs from the training points $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}$, so that the joint law of $(z,\tilde{\mathbf{z}})$ is non-degenerate. This hypothesis is not restrictive and is only needed for the proofs. Indeed, the estimators are continuous in $x$ and thus remain valid at the training points as soon as $m$ and $k$ are continuous, which we assume.

Moreover, $\mathbb{V}\left[\hat{z}-z\right]$ is a covariance matrix, and therefore SPSD whatever the weight matrix $W$. It is furthermore positive definite when $x$ is not one of the training points, since $\hat{z}-z$ is a non-degenerate linear combination of $z$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}$.

\subsection{Simple Kriging}

Simple kriging (SK) is the simplest form of kriging, and assumes that the mean function $m$ is known. \Cref{th:simple_kriging} gives a closed-form expression of the SK estimator and its associated covariance matrix, while \Cref{th:simple_kriging_conditional} shows that it coincides with the conditional distribution of $z$ given $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}$.

\begin{proposition}[Simple kriging estimator]\label{th:simple_kriging}
	Using the notations introduced in \cref{sec:kriging}, the SK estimator of $z$ and its error covariance are:
	\begin{equation}
		\left\{\begin{aligned}
		\hat{z}_{\mathrm{SK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}},m)&=m(x)+k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}\:(\tilde{\mathbf{z}}-m(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}))\\
		R_{\mathrm{SK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})&=k(x,x)+R-k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}k\left(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x\right)
		\end{aligned}\right.\label{eq:simple_kriging_equations}
	\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
	Since $m$ is known, we may assume it to be identically equal to $0$ by working with $f-m$ instead of $f$, without any loss of generality. In the centred case, \eqref{eq:error_covariance} can be developed as follows:
	\begin{equation}
		\mathbb{V}[W^\top\tilde{\mathbf{z}}-z]=W^\top G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:W-W^\top k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x)-k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:W+k(x,x)+R\label{eq:generic_kriging_variance}
	\end{equation}

	By symmetry of $G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})$, differentiating \eqref{eq:error_covariance} with respect to $W$ yields:
	\begin{equation}
		\begin{aligned}
		&\frac{\partial}{\partial W}\left(\operatorname{tr}\left(W^\top G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:W-W^\top k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x)-k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:W+k(x,x)+R\right)\right)=0\\
		\iff\quad&\frac{\partial}{\partial W}\left(\operatorname{tr}\left(W^\top G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:W\right)\right)-2\frac{\partial}{\partial W}\left(\operatorname{tr}\left(W^\top k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x)\right)\right)=0\\
		\iff\quad&2G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:W-2k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x)=0\\
		\iff\quad&W=G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x)
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}

	The SK estimator is thus:
	\begin{equation}
		\hat{z}_{\mathrm{SK}}=W^\top\tilde{\mathbf{z}}=k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{z}}
	\end{equation}
	and its corresponding covariance:
	\begin{equation}
		\begin{aligned}
		R_{\mathrm{SK}}&=W^\top G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:W-W^\top k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x)-k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:W+k(x,x)+R\\
		&=k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x)-k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x)\\
		&\qquad-k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x)+k(x,x)+R\\
		&=k(x,x)+R-k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x)
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}

	The general case is obtained by adding $m$ back into $f$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Additive query noise]
	In \Cref{th:simple_kriging}, the term $R$ appears because the noisy query $z$ is predicted rather than the field value $f(x)$. The two share the same estimator, and differ in covariance only by an additive $R$ term. Kriging the field can thus be done by subtracting $R$, or equivalently by taking $R=0$ as mentioned in the introduction to kriging.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Error covariance minimisation]
	While the SK weight matrix $W=G^{-1}k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x)$ was obtained by minimising the trace of $\mathbb{V}[\hat{z}-z]$, it minimises the whole covariance in the SPSD sense. Indeed:
	\begin{equation}
		\begin{aligned}
		\mathbb{V}[\hat{z}-z]-R_{\mathrm{SK}}&=W^\top G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:W-W^\top k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x)-k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:W+k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x)\\
		&=\left(W-G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x)\right)^\top G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\left(W-G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x)\right)\\
		&\succeq0
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	with equality if and only if $W=G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x)$ since $G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\succ0$.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Exact interpolation in the noiseless case]
	Assume that $\tilde{R}=0$, and let $i\in[\![1,\tilde{n}]\!]$. The SK estimator interpolates the data at $\tilde{x}_i$ exactly:
	\begin{equation}
		\left\{\begin{aligned}
		\hat{z}_{\mathrm{SK}}(\tilde{x}_i,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}},m)&=\tilde{z}_i\\
		R_{\mathrm{SK}}(\tilde{x}_i,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})&=R
		\end{aligned}\right.
	\end{equation}

	In particular, the error covariance reduces to the query noise $R$ alone, and vanishes when $R=0$. That degenerate case is the one set aside at the start of this section, and is reached by continuity in $x$. On the contrary, a positive training noise $\tilde{R}\succ0$ turns kriging into a smoother rather than an interpolator \cite{bib:spline-correspondence}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
	Since $\tilde{R}=0$, the Gram matrix reduces to $G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})=k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{x}})$. Let $e_i\triangleq[0,\dots,0,1,0,\dots,0]^\top$ be the $i$-th canonical basis vector of $\mathbb{R}^{\tilde{n}}$. The matrix $e_i^\top\otimes I_{d_z}$ extracts the $i$-th block of a stacked matrix, such that:
	\begin{equation}
		\left(e_i^\top\otimes I_{d_z}\right)G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})=\left(e_i^\top\otimes I_{d_z}\right)k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{x}})=k(\tilde{x}_i,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})
	\end{equation}

	The kriging weights thus reduce to this selector:
	\begin{equation}
		k(\tilde{x}_i,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}=\left(e_i^\top\otimes I_{d_z}\right)G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}=e_i^\top\otimes I_{d_z}
	\end{equation}

	Evaluating \eqref{eq:simple_kriging_equations} at $x=\tilde{x}_i$ by the continuity yields:
	\begin{equation}
		\begin{aligned}
			\hat{z}_{\mathrm{SK}}(\tilde{x}_i,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}},m)&=m(\tilde{x}_i)+k(\tilde{x}_i,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}\left(\tilde{\mathbf{z}}-m(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\right)\\
			&=m(\tilde{x}_i)+\left(e_i^\top\otimes I_{d_z}\right)\left(\tilde{\mathbf{z}}-m(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\right)\\
			&=m(\tilde{x}_i)+\tilde{z}_i-m(\tilde{x}_i)\\
			&=\tilde{z}_i
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}

	Similarly:
	\begin{equation}
		\begin{aligned}
			R_{\mathrm{SK}}(\tilde{x}_i,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})&=k(\tilde{x}_i,\tilde{x}_i)+R-k(\tilde{x}_i,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{x}_i)\\
			&=k(\tilde{x}_i,\tilde{x}_i)+R-\left(e_i^\top\otimes I_{d_z}\right)k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{x}_i)\\
			&=k(\tilde{x}_i,\tilde{x}_i)+R-k(\tilde{x}_i,\tilde{x}_i)\\
			&=R
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Gaussian conditioning interpretation]\label{th:simple_kriging_conditional}
	The conditional distribution of $z$ knowing $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}$ is Gaussian, parametrised by the SK estimator and its error covariance matrix:
	\begin{equation}
		z|\tilde{\mathbf{z}}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(\hat{z}_{\mathrm{SK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}},m),R_{\mathrm{SK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\right)
	\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
	Since they are evaluations of the same GP under an additive Gaussian white noise, $z$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}$ are jointly Gaussian. Their joint distribution is:
	\begin{equation}
		\begin{bmatrix}
		z\\\tilde{\mathbf{z}}
		\end{bmatrix}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(\begin{bmatrix}
		m(x)\\m(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})
		\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}
		k(x,x)+R&k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\\k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x)&G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})
		\end{bmatrix}\right)
	\end{equation}

	As noted at the start of this section, this joint covariance is SPD. The Gaussian conditioning formula (see \Cref{th:Gaussian_conditioning}) can thus be used to give the desired result.
\end{proof}

This result is a special case of the more general result established in \Cref{th:MMSE_estimator}. Since the unbiased hypothesis of the BLUE is automatically verified by the SK estimator, the minimisation criterion \eqref{eq:error_covariance} amounts to finding the MMSE estimator of $z$ given $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}$, which is always the conditional expectation.

\begin{remark}[Simultaneous estimation]
	The simultaneous estimation of $f$ at several query points $\mathbf{x}=(x_1,\dots,x_n)\in\mathcal{U}^n$ can be done by directly replacing $x$ with $\mathbf{x}$ in \eqref{eq:simple_kriging_equations}. In particular, the $\mathcal{O}\left(\tilde{n}^3\right)$ cost of inverting $G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})$ can be shared across the $n$ estimations whether they are performed successively or simultaneously.

	However, computing only the $n$ diagonal blocks of $R_{\mathrm{SK}}(\mathbf{x},\tilde{\mathbf{x}})$ costs $\mathcal{O}(\tilde{n}^3+n\tilde{n}^2)$, whereas forming the full matrix before discarding its off-diagonal blocks costs $\mathcal{O}(\tilde{n}^3+n\tilde{n}^2+n^2\tilde{n})$ due to the extra off-diagonal matrix products. The off-diagonal blocks describe the correlation between the estimation errors made at distinct query points, and should only be computed when that correlation is needed.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Ordinary Kriging}

Assuming the mean function to be known is a strong assumption, and can be unrealistic in practical scenarios. While an approximated model could be used instead of the true $m$, this would cause the kriging estimator to be overly confident in its estimation. Ordinary kriging (OK) extends its simple counterpart by assuming that the mean is an unknown constant $m\in\mathbb{R}^{d_z}$, and estimating it alongside the weights.

The unbiased assumption of the OK estimator yields:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
	&\mathbb{E}[\hat{z}-z]=0\\
	\iff\quad&W^\top\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\mathbf{z}}]-\mathbb{E}[z]=0\\
	\iff\quad&\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\tilde{n}}w_i^\top-I_{d_z}\right)m=0\\
	\iff\quad&C^\top W-I_{d_z}=0
	\end{aligned}\label{eq:ordinary_kriging_constraint}
\end{equation}
with:
\begin{equation}
	C\triangleq\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{n}}\otimes I_{d_z}\in\mathcal{M}_{\tilde{n}d_z,d_z}(\mathbb{R}),\qquad\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{n}}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}1\\\vdots\\1\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{\tilde{n}}
\end{equation}

The OK weights thus minimise \eqref{eq:error_covariance} under the constraint \eqref{eq:ordinary_kriging_constraint}. Using the Lagrange multiplier method \cite{bib:Lagrange-multipliers}, \Cref{th:ordinary_kriging} derives the corresponding estimator and covariance matrix, and \Cref{th:ordinary_simple_kriging_link} establishes the link between ordinary and simple kriging.

\begin{proposition}[Ordinary kriging estimator]\label{th:ordinary_kriging}
	The OK estimator of $z$ given $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}$ and its error covariance are:
	\begin{equation}
		\left\{\begin{aligned}
		\hat{z}_{\mathrm{OK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}})&=\hat{m}_{\mathrm{OK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}})+k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}\left(\tilde{\mathbf{z}}-C\:\hat{m}_{\mathrm{OK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}})\right)\\
		R_{\mathrm{OK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})&=R_{\hat{m}_{\mathrm{OK}}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})+k(x,x)+R-k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}k\left(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x\right)
		\end{aligned}\right.\label{eq:ordinary_kriging_equations}
	\end{equation}
	with:
	\begin{equation}
		\left\{\begin{aligned}
		\hat{m}_{\mathrm{OK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}})&=\left(C^\top G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}C\right)^{-1}C^\top G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{z}}\\
		R_{\hat{m}_{\mathrm{OK}}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})&=\left(I_{d_z}-k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}C\right)\left(C^\top G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}C\right)^{-1}\\
		&\qquad\left(I_{d_z}-k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}C\right)^\top
		\end{aligned}\right.
	\end{equation}
	and $R_{\hat{m}_{\mathrm{OK}}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\succeq0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
	See \cref{sec:proof_OK}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Comparison between simple and ordinary kriging]\label{th:ordinary_simple_kriging_link}
	The formulas obtained in \eqref{eq:ordinary_kriging_equations} closely resemble those of simple kriging. In fact, they can be rewritten as follows:
	\begin{equation}
		\left\{\begin{aligned}
		\hat{z}_{\mathrm{OK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}})&=\hat{z}_{\mathrm{SK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}},m_{\mathrm{OK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}}))\\
		R_{\mathrm{OK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})&=R_{\hat{m}_{\mathrm{OK}}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})+R_{\mathrm{SK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})
		\end{aligned}\right.
	\end{equation}
	where, by abuse of notation, $m_{\mathrm{OK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}})$ denotes either the estimated mean vector or its replication for each the training point $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}$, namely:
	\begin{equation}
		\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}
		m_{\mathrm{OK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}})\\\vdots\\m_{\mathrm{OK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}})
		\end{bmatrix}}_{\tilde{n}\text{ times}}=C\:m_{\mathrm{OK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}})
	\end{equation}

	Ordinary kriging can therefore be interpreted as simple kriging in which the mean $m$ is concurrently estimated from the observations. The uncertainty associated with this second estimation is captured by the covariance term $R_{\hat{m}_{\mathrm{OK}}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\succeq0$, and is added to the SK covariance.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Universal Kriging}

Universal kriging (UK) \cite{bib:universal-kriging} generalises the constant mean of ordinary kriging to a linear combination of $r_{\mathrm{UK}}\in\mathbb{N}^*$ known trend functions $g_1,\dots,g_{r_{\mathrm{UK}}}:\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}$:
\begin{equation}
	m(x)=g(x)\:m_{\mathrm{UK}}=\sum_{i=1}^{r_{\mathrm{UK}}}g_i(x)\:m_{\mathrm{UK},i}
\end{equation}
where $g(x)$ is the trend matrix:
\begin{equation}
	g(x)\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}g_1(x)&\cdots&g_{r_{\mathrm{UK}}}(x)\end{bmatrix}\otimes I_{d_z}\in\mathcal{M}_{d_z,r_{\mathrm{UK}}d_z}(\mathbb{R})
\end{equation}
and $m_{\mathrm{UK}}$ gathers the unknown coefficients $m_{\mathrm{UK},i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_z}$:
\begin{equation}
	m_{\mathrm{UK}}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}
	m_{\mathrm{UK},1}\\\vdots\\m_{\mathrm{UK},r_{\mathrm{UK}}}
	\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{\mathrm{UK}}d_z}
\end{equation}

The trend matrix at the training points stacks these evaluations:
\begin{equation}
	g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})=\begin{bmatrix}
	g(\tilde{x}_1)\\\vdots\\g(\tilde{x}_{\tilde{n}})
	\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal{M}_{\tilde{n}d_z,r_{\mathrm{UK}}d_z}(\mathbb{R})
\end{equation}
so that $m(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})=g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:m_{\mathrm{UK}}$ lies in $\mathbb{R}^{\tilde{n}d_z}$, as does $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}$.

This matrix is assumed to have full column rank, which requires at least $\tilde{n}\geq r_{\mathrm{UK}}$ training points, and the trend functions to be linearly independent once restricted to the training points. \Cref{th:universal_kriging} generalises \Cref{th:ordinary_kriging} accordingly.

\begin{proposition}[Universal kriging estimator]\label{th:universal_kriging}
	Assume that $g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})$ has full column rank. The UK estimator of $z$ given $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}$ and its error covariance are:
	\begin{equation}
		\left\{\begin{aligned}
		\hat{z}_{\mathrm{UK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}},g)&=g(x)\:m_{\mathrm{UK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}},g)+k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}\left(\tilde{\mathbf{z}}-g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:m_{\mathrm{UK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}},g)\right)\\
		R_{\mathrm{UK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},g)&=R_{\hat{m}_{\mathrm{UK}}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},g)+k(x,x)+R-k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}k\left(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},x\right)
		\end{aligned}\right.\label{eq:universal_kriging_equations}
	\end{equation}
	with:
	\begin{equation}
		\left\{\begin{aligned}
		m_{\mathrm{UK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}},g)&=\left(g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^\top G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\right)^{-1}g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^\top G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{z}}\\
		R_{\hat{m}_{\mathrm{UK}}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},g)&=\left(g(x)-k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\right)\left(g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^\top G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\right)^{-1}\\
		&\qquad\left(g(x)-k(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\right)^\top
		\end{aligned}\right.\label{eq:kriged_universal_mean}
	\end{equation}
	and $R_{\hat{m}_{\mathrm{UK}}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},g)\succeq0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
	See \cref{sec:proof_UK}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Link with ordinary and simple kriging]
	As mentioned in the beginning of this section, ordinary kriging is a special case of universal kriging, which arises when $r_{\mathrm{UK}}=1$ and $g_1\equiv1$. In this scenario, the trend matrices reduce to $g(x)=I_{d_z}$ and $g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})=C$. This, in turn, yields:
	\begin{equation}
		\left\{\begin{aligned}
		m_{\mathrm{UK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}},1)&=m_{\mathrm{OK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}})\\
		R_{\hat{m}_{\mathrm{UK}}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},1)&=R_{\hat{m}_{\mathrm{OK}}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})
		\end{aligned}\right.
	\end{equation}
	and thus:
	\begin{equation}
		\left\{\begin{aligned}
		\hat{z}_{\mathrm{UK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}},1)&=\hat{z}_{\mathrm{OK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}})\\
		R_{\mathrm{UK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},1)&=R_{\mathrm{OK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})
		\end{aligned}\right.
	\end{equation}

	Similarly to \Cref{th:ordinary_simple_kriging_link}, universal kriging can be seen as the simultaneous simple kriging from the training points and mean estimation from the trend functions. Indeed, comparing \eqref{eq:universal_kriging_equations} with \Cref{th:simple_kriging} shows that the following relation:
	\begin{equation}
		\left\{\begin{aligned}
		\hat{z}_{\mathrm{UK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}},g)&=\hat{z}_{\mathrm{SK}}\left(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}},g\:m_{\mathrm{UK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}},g)\right)\\
		R_{\mathrm{UK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},g)&=R_{\hat{m}_{\mathrm{UK}}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},g)+R_{\mathrm{SK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}})
		\end{aligned}\right.
	\end{equation}

	\Cref{th:ordinary_simple_kriging_link} is the special case $r_{\mathrm{UK}}=1$, $g_1\equiv1$ of this reading, where the estimated mean function is constant.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Conditioning under a vague trend prior]\label{th:kriging_vague_prior}
	\Cref{th:simple_kriging_conditional} interprets simple kriging as the Gaussian conditioning of $z$ knowing $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}$. While this interpretation cannot be directly extended to the OK and UK estimators, it can be recovered \textbf{TODO: à reprendre voire à mettre en proposition}

	\Cref{th:simple_kriging_conditional} reads simple kriging as a Gaussian conditioning, which ordinary and universal kriging do not admit as such, their estimators being BLUEs of an unknown mean rather than posterior expectations. That reading is recovered under a Gaussian prior $\mathcal{N}(0,B)$ on the trend coefficients, which makes the trend part of the process and amounts to simple kriging under the augmented kernel:
	\begin{equation}
		k_B(x,x')\triangleq k(x,x')+g(x)\:B\:g(x')^\top
	\end{equation}
	Letting $B^{-1}\to0$ leaves that prior flat and returns \eqref{eq:universal_kriging_equations} \cite{bib:Rasmussen-regression}. In this limit, $\mathcal{N}\left(\hat{z}_{\mathrm{UK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{z}},g),R_{\mathrm{UK}}(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},g)\right)$ is the conditional law of $z$ knowing $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}$, and \eqref{eq:ordinary_kriging_equations} follows as the case $r_{\mathrm{UK}}=1$, $g_1\equiv1$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Sequential Kriging Estimation}

\textbf{TODO: à reprendre}

So far, we have assumed that the training points were all available simultaneously, but that may not be the case in real-time navigation scenarios. For example, we might only be given training data by batch at each time step. In this section, we present a sequential formulation of kriging, and we derive ...

Kriging so far assumes the training set to be given once and for all. When observations arrive by batches, as they do along a trajectory, refactorising $G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})$ at every batch is wasteful, since the previous estimate already summarises the data it was built from. \Cref{th:kriging_update} gives the corresponding update, and \Cref{th:universal_kriging_update} shows that the same formula serves the three estimators.

\begin{proposition}[Sequential kriging update]\label{th:kriging_update}
	Let $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}}\in\mathcal{U}^{\tilde{n}_{\mathrm{new}}}$ be a batch of new training points, disjoint from $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}$, and $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{new}}$ their observations. Writing $\hat{z}$ and $R$ for the SK estimator and covariance built on $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}$ alone, their updates read:
	\begin{equation}
		\left\{\begin{aligned}
		\hat{z}_{\mathrm{new}}(x)&=\hat{z}(x)+R(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}})\:R(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}},\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}})^{-1}\left(\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{new}}-\hat{z}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}})\right)\\
		R_{\mathrm{new}}(x)&=R(x)-R(x,\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}})\:R(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}},\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}})^{-1}R(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}},x)
		\end{aligned}\right.\label{eq:kriging_update}
	\end{equation}
	where $R(\cdot,\cdot)$ denotes the error cross-covariance of the current estimator between two points.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[A Kalman correction in disguise]
	\Cref{eq:kriging_update} is the correction step of \Cref{alg:Kalman_filter}, with $R(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}},\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}})$ in the role of the innovation covariance and $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{new}}-\hat{z}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}})$ in that of the innovation. This is expected, since \Cref{th:simple_kriging_conditional} reads kriging as a Gaussian conditioning, and conditioning on a batch at once or on the union of two batches must agree.

	The cross-covariance term is what makes the update correct, and it is precisely what an earlier formulation omitted \cite{bib:kriging-update-incorrect}. Dropping it amounts to assuming $R(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}},\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}})$ diagonal, that is, new observations conditionally independent given the old ones. Measurements collected a few metres apart along a trajectory are the opposite of that \cite{bib:kriging-update}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[One update for the three estimators]\label{th:universal_kriging_update}
	\Cref{eq:kriging_update} involves the current estimator and its error covariance, never the Gram matrix. It therefore applies unchanged to ordinary and universal kriging, which \Cref{th:kriging_vague_prior} reads as simple kriging under an augmented kernel. Only the covariance fed to the update changes, from $R_{\mathrm{SK}}$ to $R_{\mathrm{OK}}$ or $R_{\mathrm{UK}}$.

	Two differences survive this unification. The UK estimator is undefined until $g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})$ has full column rank, so the recursion needs a first batch of at least $r_{\mathrm{UK}}$ points, where simple kriging accepts any number. And the vague limit is not to be implemented by substituting the augmented kernel, so that the trend coefficients are carried explicitly rather than dissolved into $R_{\mathrm{UK}}$.
\end{remark}

What such a recursion carries is the pair $\left(m_{\mathrm{UK}},M\right)$, where $M$ is the matrix inverted in \eqref{eq:kriged_universal_mean}. That matrix is the error covariance of the coefficients themselves, $R_{\hat{m}_{\mathrm{UK}}}$ being only its image at a query point. Writing $U_{\mathrm{new}}$ for the factor of \eqref{eq:kriged_universal_mean} evaluated at the new points:
\begin{equation}
	U_{\mathrm{new}}\triangleq g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}})-k(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}},\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\:G(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})^{-1}g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\in\mathcal{M}_{\tilde{n}_{\mathrm{new}}d_z,r_{\mathrm{UK}}d_z}(\mathbb{R})
\end{equation}
the batch updates them as:
\begin{equation}
	\left\{\begin{aligned}
	m_{\mathrm{UK}}^{\mathrm{new}}&=m_{\mathrm{UK}}+M\:U_{\mathrm{new}}^\top\:R_{\mathrm{UK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}})^{-1}\left(\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{new}}-\hat{z}_{\mathrm{UK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}})\right)\\
	M_{\mathrm{new}}&=M-M\:U_{\mathrm{new}}^\top\:R_{\mathrm{UK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}})^{-1}U_{\mathrm{new}}\:M
	\end{aligned}\right.\label{eq:universal_kriging_update}
\end{equation}

This is again a Kalman correction, this time on the coefficients rather than on the field, and its innovation covariance is the UK covariance at the new points:
\begin{equation}
	R_{\mathrm{UK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}})=R_{\mathrm{SK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}})+U_{\mathrm{new}}\:M\:U_{\mathrm{new}}^\top
\end{equation}
The two terms separate what the new batch teaches about the field from what it teaches about the trend, and the second is exactly the uncertainty the update consumes. In information form, $M_{\mathrm{new}}^{-1}=M^{-1}+U_{\mathrm{new}}^\top R_{\mathrm{SK}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{new}})^{-1}U_{\mathrm{new}}$, so each batch adds to the information on the trend and never removes any.

\Cref{eq:universal_kriging_update} has a reading of its own. The trend coefficients are learnt as the mission proceeds, rather than fitted once on a prior survey, and the pair $\left(m_{\mathrm{UK}},M\right)$ is then a map being built while the vehicle moves, which is the promise of simultaneous localisation and mapping.

\begin{remark}[Why this is not yet SLAM]
	The recursion above assumes the training inputs to be known, as every kriging estimator of this section does. A navigating vehicle does not know them. It would feed the update the pair $\left(\hat{x}_k,z_k\right)$, its current state estimate and its current observation, which breaks the assumption twice over.

	The state estimate is uncertain, so the training input carries an error, and input noise does not act like observation noise. More importantly, $\hat{x}_k$ was produced by a correction step that already used $z_k$. The two members of the pair are thus correlated, and the map is taught to agree with the very estimate that produced it.
\end{remark}
